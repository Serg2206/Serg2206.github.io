Expert Diagnostic Laparoscopy

The gold standard for diagnosing unexplained abdominal conditions — when ultrasound, CT and MRI give no answer.

What the procedure includes

Diagnostic laparoscopy on Karl Storz equipment is a complete inspection of the abdominal organs, with the option to proceed immediately to treatment.

1

Inspection of all abdominal and pelvic organs in Full HD

2

Biopsy of any mass, lymph node or peritoneum — under direct vision

3

Lymph node dissection for cancer staging

4

Material for histology + immunohistochemistry → accurate morphological verification

5

If indicated — immediate transition to surgical treatment

🎯 When diagnostic laparoscopy is needed
Unexplained abdominal tumor Ascites of unknown cause Suspected metastases Cancer staging Chronic abdominal pain without a diagnosis Lymph node mass Suspected peritoneal carcinomatosis Differential diagnosis of tumors
Advantage over CT/MRI-guided biopsy

In laparoscopic biopsy the surgeon sees the lesion directly, chooses the best site for sampling and controls bleeding. Diagnostic accuracy is higher than with “blind” CT-guided needle biopsies.

How it works

From Consultation to Discharge

1

Consultation with the professor — by appointment

Examination, review of imaging (CT/MRI/ultrasound) and test results. The professor determines the best method and extent of surgery, explains the details and answers all questions.

2

Preoperative assessment

Blood tests (CBC, biochemistry, coagulation), ECG, ultrasound, CT if needed. Anesthesiologist consultation. 1–3 days.

3

Surgery on Karl Storz equipment

Performed personally by Professor Sushkov. Duration depends on the extent of the procedure. Intraoperative biopsy and frozen-section histology if needed.

4

Inpatient observation

Laparoscopic operations — discharge on day 1–2. Open operations — 3–5 days. Pain relief, dressings, follow-up checks.

5

Recovery and follow-up

Laparoscopy — back to work in 7–14 days. Minor surgery — in 2–5 days. The professor is available for questions by phone and Telegram.

Frequently asked questions

Questions and Answers

Which equipment is used for laparoscopy?
MARIA Medical Center uses Karl Storz laparoscopic equipment. The specific instruments depend on the procedure.
Can a biopsy be taken during laparoscopy?
Yes. When indicated, the surgeon can obtain a tissue sample under direct visualization and send it for pathological examination.
Which hernias are treated laparoscopically and which by open surgery?
The approach is selected individually based on hernia type and size, previous operations, general condition, and surgical expertise.
Do cosmetic sutures need removal?
This depends on the suture material. Absorbable sutures usually do not require removal; follow the discharge instructions.
Are removed lesions sent for pathology?
Removed tissue is generally sent for pathological examination. The final decision depends on the procedure and clinical situation.
How can I confirm the surgical consultation terms?
Confirm current terms when booking by phone or through an available messenger.
How long does laparoscopic surgery take?
Duration depends on diagnosis, extent, and complexity. The surgeon can estimate it after evaluation.
How long is recovery after laparoscopy?
Recovery depends on the operation, patient condition, and type of work.
When can I return to work after laparoscopy?
Timing depends on procedure extent and physical demands and is advised by the surgeon.
Is general anesthesia required for laparoscopy?
Most laparoscopic procedures use general anesthesia. The anesthesiologist selects the method.
Which complications can occur after laparoscopy?
Risks depend on the procedure and patient and are discussed before surgery.

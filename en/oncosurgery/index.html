Treatment pathway

How Cancer Treatment Is Planned

⚠️ Important: if cancer is suspected, do not delay seeing a specialist. The stage of the disease determines the extent of surgery and the prognosis. The earlier, the better the outcome.
1

Consultation with a surgical oncologist — by appointment

Review of existing data (CT, MRI, biopsy, ultrasound). Staging and an individual treatment plan. Answers to all questions from the patient and family.

2

Further tests (if needed)

CT of the chest and abdomen, MRI, PET-CT, tumor markers, morphological verification. Tumor board with an oncologist, chemotherapist and radiologist when needed.

3

Preoperative preparation

Assessment of functional status, nutritional support, management of coexisting conditions. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy when indicated.

4

Surgery

Radical surgery following oncological principles: clear margins, lymph node dissection, prevention of tumor spread. Intraoperative morphology.

5

Postoperative follow-up

Regular follow-up, check-up examinations and decisions on adjuvant treatment. Professor Sushkov personally oversees the patient at every stage.

Frequently asked questions

Questions About Oncologic Surgery

Is an oncologist’s referral required for surgical consultation?
No. A referral is not mandatory. Bring any available examination results.
Can cancer be treated without surgery?
This depends on tumor type, stage, and sensitivity to systemic or radiation therapy. The decision should be multidisciplinary.
What should I bring to an oncologic surgeon consultation?
Bring CT or MRI reports and images, biopsy results, laboratory tests, ultrasound reports, and discharge summaries. The physician can arrange missing examinations.
How can I obtain the cost of cancer surgery?
Cost depends on diagnosis, procedure extent, materials, and additional treatment stages. An estimate is possible after evaluation.
How quickly can surgery be scheduled?
Timing depends on urgency, patient condition, completed tests, and operating-room availability. Emergency symptoms require immediate care.
Can cancer surgery be performed laparoscopically?
For some tumors, a minimally invasive approach is appropriate and guideline-concordant. Suitability depends on stage, location, and general condition.
How should I prepare for tumor surgery?
Preparation is individualized and may include tests, imaging, anesthesia assessment, and medication adjustment. Do not stop medicines without medical advice.
Is drug treatment needed after surgery?
The decision depends on stage, pathology, biomarkers, and general condition.
How is treatment prognosis assessed?
Prognosis is individual and is discussed after complete staging.
How often are follow-up visits needed after cancer surgery?
The oncologist prepares an individual follow-up plan based on current guidance.
What should I do if metastases are found?
Consult the oncology team to assess all available treatment options.
